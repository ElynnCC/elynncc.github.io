Trustworthy AI for Business

Pricing, finance, market design, generative AI, and agentic AI

This stream builds AI systems that businesses can rely on, and applies them to decisions in finance, accounting, economics, and operations. These are settings that demand learning under uncertainty, structured high-dimensional information, adaptation to heterogeneity, and operation under latency, strategic interaction, and operational constraints. Two connected directions. The first is reliable AI systems: language models whose answers are invariant to logically equivalent rephrasing, interpretable signatures of reasoning inside LLMs, uncertainty quantification for generative models and tensor-structured networks, and agentic workflows with provable convergence and latency guarantees. The second is AI for business decisions: dynamic pricing and assortment under cross-market heterogeneity, market design and extractable value in financial markets, evidence-grounded audit-risk reasoning from SEC filings, and decision systems for economics and trade. The two directions share one premise: a model used for consequential decisions should be consistent, calibrated, and fast, not only accurate on a benchmark.

Representative work: Building Logic-invariant LLMs through Logical Structure (NeurIPS, 2026), Latency-Aware Multi-Agent Workflow Learning (NeurIPS, 2026), Learning to Hand Off: Provably Convergent Workflow Learning (2026), Small-World Connectivity as a Signature of Reasoning Performance (2026), Quantifying Epistemic Uncertainty in Diffusion Models (AISTATS, 2026), Seeing Through the Brain (ICLR 2026, Oral); VERA-8B: Evidence-Grounded Audit Risk Reasoning from SEC Filings (2026), Dynamic Contextual Pricing with Doubly Non-Parametric Random Utility Models (Management Science, 2026), Transfer Learning for Contextual Joint Assortment-Pricing (2026), Maximal Extractable Value in Batch Auctions (ACM EC, 2025), ACT-Tensor (ICAIF, 2025). When Generative AI Is Guessing: Separating What a Model Knows from What It Makes Up

Generative models are random by design, so their outputs do not reveal when the model is out of its depth. In an AISTATS 2026 paper we isolate the uncertainty that comes from the model not knowing, propagate it through the generation process, and use it to filter out the outputs the model had no basis for.
